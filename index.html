Walking trails tell different stories.

Trail networks differ in where they are located, how difficult they are and how far they extend. This visualisation explores regional walking-track patterns in New South Wales before taking a closer look at individual routes in Hobart.

5,087 NSW walking and hiking track sections analysed

8 NSW NPWS regions compared

Sources: NSW NPWS, City of Hobart and ABS. Data cleaned and transformed for visualisation.

THE TAKEAWAY

One trail network. Many different patterns.

Walking trails cannot be understood through a single measure. Location reveals concentration, classifications describe difficulty, and track length shows the scale of regional networks.

NSW demonstrates strong regional differences, while the Hobart case study shows how those broader patterns translate into individual routes.

Why & Who

This visualisation helps general readers and outdoor recreation users understand how walking trails vary by location, scale and difficulty.

What

The project uses NSW walking-track, NPWS branch, Australian state boundary and Hobart recreational trail datasets.

How

Route maps show individual trails, proportional-symbol and hotspot maps show spatial distribution, choropleth maps compare normalised track density across regions,and statistical charts compare track count, class and length.
